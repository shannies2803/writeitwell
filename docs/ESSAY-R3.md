# Round 3 essays — extra notes (read ESSAY-SPEC.md first; it still applies)

The two children using the site most are in P1 and P3, so this round leans to LOWER and MIDDLE levels. Lower-level essays must be genuinely easy to read for a 6–8-year-old: short sentences, everyday words, one clear feeling journey, 1–2 lines of natural dialogue, 3–5 short paragraphs. Middle-level essays should be a clear step up but still a comfortable read for a P3 child.

An audit of the existing 200 essays per language found these habits to AVOID this round:
- English: opening with a question ("Have you ever…?") — used 13 times already; do NOT use it. Ending with "I learnt that…" / "lesson" — used in 37 essays; at most ONE of your 10 essays may state a lesson outright. End instead with an image, a callback to the opening, an action, a line of dialogue, or a quiet thought.
- English overused phrases: "my heart sank", "my cheeks burned", "grinned from ear to ear", "butterflies in my stomach" — don't use them. Find fresher show-don't-tell.
- Chinese: endings with "从那以后" (21 times) and "我终于明白" (13 times) — use each at most once across your 10. Avoid "心里像…" more than once; avoid "七上八下"/"热锅上的蚂蚁".
- Openings: vary across your 10 — sound, dialogue, action, setting, flashback, feeling, a surprising statement. Never "One day" / "有一天". No two of yours may start the same way.
- Names: vary them (Chinese, Malay, Indian, Eurasian and other Singaporean names); avoid overusing Priya, Ethan, Marcus, Rachel, Ah Ma.

Titles/opening lines of every existing essay are in titles-en.txt / titles-zh.txt — skim them and do NOT repeat a plot.

New categories this round (keys must match exactly):
- performing — music lessons and exams, concerts, choir, band, dance, drama, stage fright, practising
- science — experiments, robotics, building and testing things, curiosity about nature, a science fair, a mistake that leads to discovery
- travel — family trips abroad and in Singapore (snow, a long flight, getting lost briefly in a big station, trying new food, a language barrier)
- festivals — Chinese New Year, Hari Raya, Deepavali, Christmas, Mid-Autumn, birthdays, school celebrations
- responsibility — chores, caring for a pet or sibling, keeping a promise, a class duty, being trusted with something

IDs: use exactly the id range given in your brief so files never clash.
